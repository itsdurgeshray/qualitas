The cytometer isn't the problem.

For decades, the field optimized optics, lasers, detectors and software — while relying on biological references that naturally drift over time.

Controls were always the bottleneck

Every donor’s blood is different. Every cell line changes with passage. The reference itself becomes unstable — making it nearly impossible to distinguish true biological change from technical noise.

Hardware couldn't solve a biology problem

Better instruments improved measurement precision, but not standardization. If the control changes more than the assay, the assay loses meaning.

Change what you measure against

Slingshot Cell Mimics are synthetic hydrogel particles engineered to scatter and stain like real cells — while remaining shelf-stable, non-biohazardous and manufactured to spec.
