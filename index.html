The Challenge

The cytometer isn't the problem

For two decades, the field has poured money into better lasers, more detectors, smarter unmixing. The instruments became great. The reproducibility, didn't.

You can't fix that with optics. You fix it by changing what you measure against.

The reason is as old as cytometry itself: controls, controls, controls. Inherently, every donor's blood is different and every cell line drifts with passage, simply because of the heterogenicity of living things. Using these shifting controls as references, across time and multiple sites, is a biological problem which cannot be solved with a hardware solution.

The Problem You Already Know

If you run flow,
you've lived this.

How to differentiate between true biological changes in your subjects and the inevitable technical noise of the instrument and reagents over time?

It doesn't. When the control changes more than the assay, the assay can't tell you anything.

The Solution

What if the reference didn't move

Traditional reference
Polystyrene Bead

A golf ball — round, dense, optical. It doesn't scatter or stain like a living cell, so it cannot serve as a meaningful biological reference.

Biological control
Real Cell

A tomato — soft, complex, perishable. Scatters and stains right. But every donor is different, every lot drifts. You cannot run the same sample twice.

A polystyrene bead is a golf ball, round, dense, optical. A real cell is a tomato, soft, complex, perishable. You want something that scatters and stains like the tomato, without the mush.

That's a cell mimic. A synthetic, hydrogel particle engineered to behave like a cell on the cytometer — same forward and side scatter, cell-like autofluorescence, biomarkers at the densities you choose — but manufactured to spec, shelf-stable, and non-biohazardous.

In a 2026 multi-platform validation, TBNK Mimic™ ran lymphocyte subsets at ≤5% CV across operators, instruments, and days, with intra-assay precision under 3% CV on every subset. Across three cytometer platforms — Cytek Aurora, Cytek Northern Lights, BD FACSLyric — and three commercial TBNK kits, the major lymphocyte populations held within 2.2% CV. (Gunturu et al., Cytometry Part A, 2026.)

The instrument didn't get better. The reference stopped moving.

Everything else follows from treating the reference as a manufacturing problem instead of a biology problem:

  • Lot 47 equals lot 48 equals lot 49.
  • Up to 36 months of shelf life. Most products ship at room temperature.
  • No biohazard. No BSL-2. No autoclave bag at the end.
  • No donor variability. No supply fragility. No "we ran out."
  • The same particle behaves the same way in Boston, Basel, and Tokyo.

That last one is the whole point.
